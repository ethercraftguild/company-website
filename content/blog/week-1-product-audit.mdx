## Why Audit Before You Build

Most engagements that go wrong do not go wrong during implementation. They go wrong before it, when assumptions about the existing system turn out to be optimistic: the dependency tree nobody updated, the deployment only one person understands, the authentication flow held together by convention.

A week-1 audit exists to replace those assumptions with a written record. You cannot scope what you have not read.

## What Gets Examined

**The codebase.** Architecture and module boundaries, test coverage and what it actually exercises, dependency freshness and known vulnerabilities, code patterns that slow down new contributors. We read it the way a new senior hire would — because that is effectively what we are about to become.

**The deployment path.** How code reaches production, who can trigger it, what happens when it fails, and how long recovery takes. A deployment only one person understands is a single point of failure wearing a process costume.

**The dependency and secret surface.** Third-party services, API keys, access scopes, and data flows. This is where compliance exposure usually hides: analytics firing before consent, overly broad cloud permissions, credentials committed where they should never have been.

**The backlog and the roadmap.** What the team says is next, weighed against what the system can currently support. The gap between those two things is the actual scope of the engagement.

## What You Receive

A written findings document, ranked by risk and effort — not a slide deck of impressions. Each finding states what we observed, why it matters, and what fixing it would take. The document is yours unconditionally: if you take it to another vendor, it still did its job.

Alongside it comes a scoped proposal for the work itself, priced against the findings rather than against a sales target.

## What It Does Not Include

It is not a penetration test — that is a separate engagement with a different methodology. It is not a rewrite recommendation by default; most systems we audit need targeted repair, not a fresh start, and we say so when that is the case. And it is not a commitment device: there is no obligation to continue, and the audit fee does not lock you into anything.

## The Point

Engineering trust is built on specifics. A week of reading produces specifics — named files, named risks, named costs — that any competent team can verify independently. That is the whole offer.
