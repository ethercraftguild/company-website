## The Question Is Team Shape, Not Framework

When a 20-person company asks whether to build in Flutter or go native, the underlying question is usually: how many mobile engineers can we afford to hire, retain, and keep busy for the next three years?

Native development means two codebases, two release pipelines, and in practice two engineers minimum — one per platform — before you count design, backend, and QA. Flutter means one codebase and one release pipeline, which a single senior engineer can own end to end for a product at this scale.

That is the whole cost argument. Everything else is detail.

## Where Flutter Wins for Small Teams

- **One engineer instead of two.** For a company with twenty people total, the difference between staffing one mobile seat and two is the difference between shipping and stalling.
- **One release train.** App Store and Play Store submissions, review rejections, and hotfixes all happen once. Release overhead roughly halves, because there is half as much to release.
- **Design consistency for free.** A single widget tree renders identically on both platforms, so the design QA burden that normally falls on the founder disappears.

## Where Native Still Wins

Flutter is not the answer for every product. Go native when:

- Your product depends on platform-specific behavior that Flutter only reaches through plugins — background processing, complex Bluetooth flows, or OS-level integrations that change every release.
- You already employ native engineers with deep platform knowledge. Rewriting a working native app in Flutter to save money is how you spend money.
- Your roadmap is iOS-first or Android-first for strategic reasons, and the second platform is a distant maybe. Building natively for one platform beats building cross-platform for two you do not need.

## The Maintenance Horizon Nobody Prices In

The cheapest app to build is rarely the cheapest app to own. Two native codebases drift apart over time: dependencies update on different schedules, platform APIs deprecate asymmetrically, and eventually a feature exists on one platform with no equivalent on the other. Reconciling that drift is the hidden line item in every native budget.

A single Flutter codebase cannot drift from itself. That does not make it free to maintain — framework upgrades and breaking changes are real — but the maintenance surface is one surface, and one surface is auditable.

## How We Scope It

Our mobile engagements start with the same question this post asks: what does your team look like, and what does your roadmap demand? If the answer points to Flutter, we build in Flutter. If it points to native, we say so — including when that means recommending against us.

That scoping conversation is part of every engagement, and it is written down before implementation starts.
